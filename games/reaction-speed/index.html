QUICK FACTS

What changes your reaction time?

Focus matters. Notifications, conversations, and even looking away for a moment can slow your response.

Your device matters too. Screen refresh rate and touch delay can slightly affect the number, so compare results on the same phone.

Practice helps. Your later rounds may be faster because your brain already knows what signal to expect.

This game is made for fun and friendly competition—not as a medical or professional reaction assessment.

How Fast Are Your Reactions?

Reaction Speed is a simple skill game that measures how quickly you respond to a visual signal.

The challenge sounds easy: wait for the signal and react as quickly as possible. The difficult part is staying ready without clicking too early.

Because a single lucky round can be misleading, the game uses five timed rounds rather than only one attempt.

How the Test Works

Each round asks you to wait until the screen gives you the signal to react. Your response time is measured for that round.

After five rounds, the game shows your average reaction time as well as your fastest attempt. Your final result is placed into one of five descriptive performance bands.

Reaction time can vary from attempt to attempt. Your device, browser, attention and even how you hold your phone can affect the number, so this game should be treated as a casual challenge rather than a scientific measurement.

Challenge Your Friends

Try the game more than once and see whether you can improve your average without jumping early.

You can also share the challenge with friends and compare your fastest rounds. Sometimes the person who talks the most about having quick reflexes is not actually the fastest.
